# Condition 1: Onboarding email only

Part of the channel comparison: [01 email](01-email-only.md) · [02 email + PDF](02-email-plus-pdf.md) · [03 email + PDF + video + context .md](03-email-pdf-video-md.md). Written 2026-09-27.

## What this condition is in our project
The newcomer gets one email (`email-newcomer.md` body, generated by `pipeline_tools.py guide`): who they are, start date, manager/buddy, first steps. No attachment, no video, no context folder.

## Evidence
| Finding | Source | Fit to our case |
|---|---|---|
| Only **22%** of information in an org-wide bulk email was retained by employees (University of Minnesota, 317 participants). | [arXiv 2308.09167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09167) | Proxy. Bulk email, not a personal onboarding email. Personalised mail from a manager likely does better; no number found. |
| Recipients often "open and rapidly discard" emails; open rate is a misleading success metric. | [arXiv 2006.16508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16508) | Same proxy caveat. |
| Hospital staff email: ~21-23% opened the linked content after 90 days, >70% of those within 3 days. | [PMC2274791](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2274791/) | Link click-through, not comprehension. Suggests: anything that must be read should not sit behind a click. |
| Onboarding docs fail through **cognitive overload** (dense, fragmented, inconsistent), not through channel alone. | [arXiv 2605.19174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19174) | Applies directly: an email that tries to carry the whole pack becomes a wall of text. |
| Mentoring / a named person to ask is a key factor in effective onboarding. | [arXiv 1311.1334](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.1334) | Email is good at this one thing: naming the buddy and the first contact. |

## Strengths
- Zero friction: nothing to open, install or play. Works on any device.
- Best for *time-critical, low-volume* facts: start date, where to be, who to contact, first 3 actions.
- Searchable, quotable, forwardable to the buddy.

## Weaknesses
- Cannot carry the technical pack (architecture, timeline table, issues list) without overload. Trimming it drops the exact content the PoC exists to deliver.
- No structure signalling beyond headings; no diagrams (Mayer's text+diagram advantage is unavailable in plain email).
- Retention evidence for email is weak (see 22% above). Expect it to be skimmed once and not revisited.

## Verdict for the PoC
Keep the email, but as a **cover note**: 5-8 lines, pointing to the PDF/context. Do not use it as the primary teaching channel.

## Hypotheses (untested, mark as such in any write-up)
- H1a: after email only, a newcomer can state their start date, manager and buddy (high recall) but not the request flow or the top issue (low recall).
- H1b: time to first successful run is longest of the three conditions because run instructions are compressed.

## Gaps
No study found that measures *technical* onboarding via email alone. All email figures above are from general workplace/health communication.
